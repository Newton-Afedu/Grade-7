Become an internet safety detective! Spot tricky downloads, help others online, and make safe choices.

🔎 Tell safe download websites from risky ones.

ACTIVITY 01 · 5 MINUTES

🎮 Would you click it?

You want to play EA SPORTS FC. A search result promises: “EA SPORTS FC FREE DOWNLOAD — FULL GAME, NO PAYMENT!”

What would you do?

Discuss your choice with a partner: Does appearing on Google make a site safe? What evidence would change your mind?

Think–Pair–Share

What makes an offer seem too good to be true? Can a website look real but still be unsafe?

🧠 Bonus: Does a padlock mean a site is safe?

No. A padlock means the connection is encrypted, but a scam website can also have a padlock. Check the website address and who made the software.

ACTIVITY 03 · 8 MINUTES

⚠️ What's the risk?

Alex installs a free copy of a paid game from an unknown website. The computer slows down, advertisements pop up, and someone tries to access Alex's gaming account.

In your group, guess what each word means. Open the cards to see whether you were right.

🦠 Malware

Harmful software that can damage a device or steal information.

🎣 Phishing

A fake message or website tries to trick you into sharing a password or other private information.

🔒 Ransomware

A harmful program locks files and asks for money to unlock them.

🧩 Unwanted software

Programs you did not ask for may appear on your computer.

ACTIVITY 04 · 15 MINUTES

💬 Cyberbullying: What would you do?

Discuss: Can someone be bullied without ever meeting the bully face-to-face?

Cyberbullying means using phones, games, messages or websites to repeatedly hurt, frighten or embarrass someone. A single serious harmful message also needs to be reported.

Choose your group's scenario:

Scenario 1 · The group chat

Students repeatedly share embarrassing pictures of a classmate in a group chat. Some react with laughing emojis; others stay silent.

Scenario 2 · The online game

Every time Sam joins an online game, the same players send mean messages and say Sam cannot join their team.

Scenario 3 · The fake account

Someone makes an account pretending to be another student and posts unkind messages using that student’s name.

Your group challenge

  1. What makes this cyberbullying?
  2. How might the person feel?
  3. What could the student do to get help?
  4. What could a friend who sees it do?
  5. Which trusted adult could help?

Prepare a 60-second explanation or respectful role-play. Do not act out abusive language or use real classmates' names.

💡 Reveal supportive actions after discussion

Do not retaliate. Tell a trusted adult, use the report and block tools, and keep evidence if it is safe to do so. Do not forward hurtful content. If someone is in danger, get adult help immediately.
